## Problem

Compare the standard basis

$$
E=((1,0),(0,1))
$$

with the basis

$$
B=(b_1,b_2),\qquad b_1=(1,1),\quad b_2=(1,-1).
$$

Consider the vectors

$$
p=(6,6),\qquad q=(4,-4),\qquad r=(8,2).
$$

1. Write the $E$-coordinates and $B$-coordinates of all three vectors.
2. Reconstruct each vector from its $B$-coordinates.
3. Compare the number and size of nonzero coefficients in both descriptions.
4. Decide which basis is better adapted to describing $p$ and $q$, and justify the choice.
5. Decide whether the same conclusion is equally clear for $r$.
6. Create one additional vector whose description is especially simple in basis $B$ and one whose standard coordinates are simpler.
7. Formulate a practical criterion for saying that a basis is adapted to a family of vectors.

<div class="required-record" markdown>

### Required record

Include a two-basis comparison table, all reconstruction checks and a justified criterion.

</div>

<div class="verification-box" markdown>

### Verification

Every $B$-coordinate pair must reconstruct the original standard-coordinate vector.
