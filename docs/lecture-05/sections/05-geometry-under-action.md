## What happens to lines, grids and shapes? {#geometry-under-action}

A matrix acts on every point of an object. The panel shows each original object together with its image.

For this panel we use the shear

$$
A=
\begin{bmatrix}
1&1\\
0&1
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

### A line is transformed through one point and one direction

A line written as

$$
\mathbf x(t)=\mathbf p+t\mathbf v
$$

becomes

$$
A\mathbf x(t)=A\mathbf p+tA\mathbf v.
$$

Thus:

- $\mathbf p$ moves to $A\mathbf p$;
- the direction $\mathbf v$ moves to $A\mathbf v$;
- the transformed line passes through $A\mathbf p$ and follows direction $A\mathbf v$.

### Parallel lines receive one common new direction

Every line in a parallel family uses the same direction $\mathbf v$. After applying $A$, every image line uses the direction $A\mathbf v$.

### A polygon is transformed by its vertices

For a triangle with vertices $\mathbf p$, $\mathbf q$ and $\mathbf r$, calculate

$$
A\mathbf p,\qquad A\mathbf q,\qquad A\mathbf r,
$$

then reconnect the output vertices in the same order.

### The unit square shows the two matrix columns at once

The unit square is generated by $\mathbf e_1$ and $\mathbf e_2$. Its image is generated by

$$
A\mathbf e_1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
A\mathbf e_2.
$$

These vectors are the two columns of $A$ and form the sides of the transformed parallelogram.

!!! note "Area will be studied later"
    The transformed parallelogram may have a different area and orientation. The numerical area factor and its sign will be introduced with the determinant in Lecture 07.
