## What happens to lines, grids and shapes? {#geometry-under-action}

A matrix acts on every point, but students should not be expected to reconstruct the whole picture from formulas alone. The panel below keeps the original object and its image visible together.

For this panel we use the shear

$$
A=
\begin{bmatrix}
1&1\\
0&1
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

### A line is transformed through one point and one direction

A line written as

$$
\mathbf x(t)=\mathbf p+t\mathbf v
$$

becomes

$$
A\mathbf x(t)=A\mathbf p+tA\mathbf v.
$$

The picture should be read in exactly the same way as the formula:

- $\mathbf p$ moves to $A\mathbf p$;
- the direction $\mathbf v$ moves to $A\mathbf v$;
- the transformed line passes through $A\mathbf p$ and follows direction $A\mathbf v$.

### Parallel lines receive one common new direction

Every line in a parallel family uses the same direction $\mathbf v$. After applying $A$, every one of them uses the direction $A\mathbf v$. The **Parallel lines** view shows the whole family before and after the shear.

### A polygon is transformed by its vertices

For a triangle with vertices $\mathbf p$, $\mathbf q$ and $\mathbf r$, calculate

$$
A\mathbf p,\qquad A\mathbf q,\qquad A\mathbf r,
$$

then reconnect the output vertices in the same order. The **Triangle** view shows both polygons together, so the formula is tied to a visible construction rather than to three isolated calculations.

### The unit square shows the two matrix columns at once

The unit square is generated by $\mathbf e_1$ and $\mathbf e_2$. Its image is therefore generated by

$$
A\mathbf e_1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
A\mathbf e_2.
$$

These are precisely the two columns of $A$. In the **Unit square** view, the two coloured arrows form the sides of the transformed parallelogram.

!!! principle "Never describe a transformed structure without showing it"
    A useful note should place the formula and the corresponding drawing next to one another. For lines and polygons, the student should see the original object, the transformed object and the points or directions used to construct it.

!!! note "Area belongs to Lecture 07"
    The parallelogram visibly changes its area, but the numerical area factor and its sign will be studied with the determinant in Lecture 07. Here the only goal is to see how the two column vectors generate the transformed square.
