## Task

Two coordinate systems have the same origin, axis directions and orientation. In the second system, one coordinate unit has length two units of the first system.

In the first system,

$$
A=(6,-4),\qquad B=(-2,8),\qquad C=(5,1).
$$

1. Find the coordinates of $A$, $B$ and $C$ in the second system.
2. Find the first-system coordinates of a point whose second-system coordinates are $(3,-2)$.
3. Compute the coordinate differences from $A$ to $B$ in both systems.
4. Compute the Euclidean distance from $A$ to $B$ using the first-system coordinates.
5. Compute the numerical distance using the second-system coordinates, then convert it to the physical length measured in first-system units.

### Required record

Include a coordinate-conversion table and both distance calculations.

</div>

<div class="verification-box" markdown>

### Verification

Explain in one sentence why the numerical coordinates and numerical distance change, although the geometric points and physical distance do not.
