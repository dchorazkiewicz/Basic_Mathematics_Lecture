## Problem

In a coordinate system $S$, consider the triangle

$$
A=(0,0),\qquad B=(6,0),\qquad C=(2,4).
$$

A second system $S'$ has:

- the same axis directions and orientation;
- origin $O'=(2,0)$ in system $S$;
- a unit length equal to two units of system $S$.

Prepare a documented comparison of the triangle in the two systems.

1. Determine the coordinates of $A$, $B$ and $C$ in $S'$.
2. Draw both coordinate systems on one diagram while keeping the geometric triangle fixed.
3. Compute the three side lengths using coordinates from $S$.
4. Compute the numerical side lengths using coordinates from $S'$.
5. Convert the numerical $S'$ lengths back to physical lengths measured in units of $S$.
6. Identify which entries in your calculations depend on the coordinate system and which geometric facts remain invariant.
7. Write a short conclusion explaining the statement: “the representation changed, not the triangle”.

<div class="required-record" markdown>

### Required record

Your solution must contain:

- one combined diagram;
- a coordinate table for both systems;
- a side-length table for both systems;
- a list of dependent and invariant quantities;
- a conclusion of four to six sentences.

</div>

<div class="verification-box" markdown>

### Verification

Reconstruct the original coordinates from the $S'$ coordinates and verify one side length in both descriptions.
