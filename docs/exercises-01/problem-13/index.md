## Problem

Let

$$
P=(x_1,y_1),\qquad Q=(x_2,y_2).
$$

Prepare a concise derivation of the Euclidean distance formula.

1. Draw $P$ and $Q$ in a generic position and complete a right triangle whose hypotenuse is $PQ$.
2. Label the horizontal and vertical changes using coordinate differences.
3. Apply the Pythagorean theorem and derive
   $$
   d(P,Q)=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}.
   $$
4. Explain why absolute values are not required inside the final squared terms.
5. Show algebraically that
   $$
   d(P,Q)=d(Q,P).
   $$
6. Show that translating both points by the same coordinate change $(a,b)$ does not change their distance.
7. Illustrate the general argument with one numerical example of your own choice.

<div class="required-record" markdown>

### Required record

Produce a short mathematical note containing a labelled generic diagram, the derivation, the two properties above and one checked numerical example.

</div>

<div class="verification-box" markdown>

### Verification

Every symbol appearing in the derivation must be identified on the diagram. The numerical example must be checked both by the formula and by the triangle side lengths.
