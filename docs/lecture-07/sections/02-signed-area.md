## The sign records orientation {#signed-area}

Area alone cannot distinguish a reflection from the identity. Both preserve ordinary area:

$$
\text{area}=1.
$$

But they do not preserve the order of the two basis directions.

For the identity matrix,

$$
\mathbf e_1
\longmapsto
\begin{bmatrix}1\\0\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\mathbf e_2
\longmapsto
\begin{bmatrix}0\\1\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Turning from the first column to the second column is counterclockwise.

For the reflection

$$
R=
\begin{bmatrix}
1&0\\
0&-1
\end{bmatrix},
$$

we have

$$
R\mathbf e_1=
\begin{bmatrix}1\\0\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
R\mathbf e_2=
\begin{bmatrix}0\\-1\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Now the order is reversed. The area is still $1$, but the orientation has changed.

We record both facts with one signed number.

!!! definition "Determinant as signed area multiplier"
    The **determinant** of a $2\times2$ matrix $A$, written $\det A$, is the signed area of the parallelogram generated by the columns of $A$.

    - $|\det A|$ is the ordinary area multiplier;
    - $\det A>0$ means orientation is preserved;
    - $\det A<0$ means orientation is reversed;
    - $\det A=0$ means the image has no area.

For the examples already visible in the panel:

$$
\det I=1,
\qquad
\det R=-1.
$$

The minus sign does **not** mean negative ordinary area. It records the reversal of orientation.
