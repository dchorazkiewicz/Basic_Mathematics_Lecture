## Start with the unit square {#area-before-formula}

Lecture 05 showed that the two columns of a matrix are the images of the basis vectors. Those two columns also determine the image of the unit square.

The unit square has vertices

$$
\mathbf 0,
\qquad
\mathbf e_1,
\qquad
\mathbf e_1+\mathbf e_2,
\qquad
\mathbf e_2.
$$

After applying a matrix $A$, the four vertices become

$$
\mathbf 0,
\qquad
A\mathbf e_1,
\qquad
A\mathbf e_1+A\mathbf e_2,
\qquad
A\mathbf e_2.
$$

Therefore the image is the parallelogram generated by the two columns of $A$.

Begin with a simple stretch:

$$
A=
\begin{bmatrix}
2&0\\
0&1
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Its columns are

$$
A\mathbf e_1=
\begin{bmatrix}2\\0\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
A\mathbf e_2=
\begin{bmatrix}0\\1\end{bmatrix}.
$$

The unit square becomes a rectangle of width $2$ and height $1$, so its area becomes

$$
2\cdot1=2.
$$

The matrix has multiplied every area by $2$.

Now select **Shear**. The square changes shape, but its base and height still give area $1$. Shape change and area change are different questions.

!!! principle "Measure the image of the unit square"
    A linear transformation applies the same area multiplier everywhere. To find that multiplier, it is enough to inspect the image of one unit square.
