Problem 12 — Designing a coordinate system

Damian Chorążkiewicz

← Exercise set 01 Lecture 01

Problem

A rectangular room is \(8\) metres long and \(6\) metres wide.

  • The centre of a door lies on one of the \(6\)-metre walls, \(1\) metre from the nearest corner.
  • The centre of a window lies on the opposite \(6\)-metre wall, \(2\) metres from the nearest corner.
  • The centre of a desk is \(3\) metres from one \(8\)-metre wall and \(2\) metres from one \(6\)-metre wall.

Choose a coordinate system that makes the description of the room convenient.

  1. Draw the room and label the four corners, the door centre, the window centre and the desk centre.
  2. State explicitly your choice of origin, axis directions, positive orientation and unit length.
  3. Assign coordinates to all labelled points.
  4. Compute the distance from the desk centre to the door centre and to the window centre.
  5. Introduce a second sensible coordinate system and rewrite all coordinates in it.
  6. Compare the two descriptions and explain which system is more convenient for which calculation.
  7. State any geometric ambiguity in the verbal description and explain how your diagram resolves it.

Required record

Prepare a one-page mathematical plan containing two labelled diagrams, two coordinate tables, the distance calculations and a short justification of both coordinate choices.

Verification

Check that every labelled point lies on the correct wall or at the prescribed distances from the walls.
