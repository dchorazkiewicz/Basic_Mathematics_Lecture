Exercise Set 01

The set follows the same division that will later be used in the student workbook:

  • Problems 01–10 — required practice: short calculations, plotting, substitution and direct verification;
  • Problems 11–20 — developing work: argument, comparison of representations, construction of diagrams and preparation of concise mathematical notes.

Each problem has its own page. The present pages contain the final problem statements for the course design stage; separate editable solution files will be created only when the forkable student workbook is prepared.

Problems 01–10 — required practice

Problem 01 — Plotting points and reading coordinates Plot five points, identify quadrants and axes, and reflect points across the coordinate axes.

Problem 02 — Changing the origin Describe the same points after translating the origin while keeping axes, orientation and unit unchanged.

Problem 03 — Changing the unit scale Convert coordinates when one new unit equals two old units and check that geometry is unchanged.

Problem 04 — Distance from a right triangle Compute a Euclidean distance and connect every term in the formula with a drawn right triangle.

Problem 05 — Recovering an unknown coordinate Use a prescribed distance to find all possible values of an unknown coordinate.

Problem 06 — Generating a graph from a function Build a value table, generate points and draw the graph of a simple linear function.

Problem 07 — Testing membership in an equation Use substitution to decide whether given points belong to two generated sets.

Problem 08 — A circle as a relation Find all outputs associated with selected inputs and see why a circle is not one global function.

Problem 09 — A parametrized segment Generate points from a parameter, draw the segment and verify a Cartesian condition.

Problem 10 — Intersections and inequalities Find an elementary intersection and classify points in a region described by two inequalities.

Problems 11–20 — developing work

Problem 11 — The same geometry in two coordinate systems Document how coordinates change while distances and the geometric triangle remain invariant.

Problem 12 — Designing a coordinate system Choose an origin, axes, orientation and scale for a simple room plan and justify the choices.

Problem 13 — Documenting the distance formula Prepare a short derivation from a right triangle and explain the role of coordinate differences.

Problem 14 — From fixed distance to a circle equation Derive and interpret the equation of a circle from its geometric definition.

Problem 15 — One circle, three descriptions Compare a relation, two function branches and a parametrization of the same circle.

Problem 16 — Constructing a segment parametrization Build a general parametrization from two endpoints and verify its geometric meaning.

Problem 17 — Two parametrizations of the same curve Show that two different parameter rules generate the same unit circle but traverse it differently.

Problem 18 — Intersections as simultaneous conditions Compare a line that cuts, touches or misses a circle and justify each case algebraically.

Problem 19 — Describing a region by inequalities Draw and document the intersection of a disk and a half-plane, including its boundary points.

Problem 20 — Equations generate geometry Create a one-page mathematical note combining a table, graph, parametrization, inequality and verification.

Structure of the set

The distribution is intentional:

Lecture component Required practice Developing work
Object and representation 01–03 11–12
Distance and coordinate calculation 04–05 13–14
Equations generating sets 06–08 15
Parametrization 09 16–17
Intersections and regions 10 18–20

Every developing problem requires a visible mathematical product: an annotated diagram, a comparison table, a derivation, a verified construction or a concise explanatory note.
