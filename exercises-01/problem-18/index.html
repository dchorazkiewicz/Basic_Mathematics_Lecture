Problem 18 — Intersections as simultaneous conditions

Damian Chorążkiewicz

← Exercise set 01 Lecture 01

Problem

Consider the circle

\[ x^2+y^2=4 \]

and the horizontal family of lines

\[ y=c. \]
  1. Find all intersection points for \(c=1\).
  2. Find all intersection points for \(c=2\).
  3. Show that there are no real intersection points for \(c=3\).
  4. Draw the three lines and the circle on one diagram.
  5. For a general real number \(c\), substitute \(y=c\) into the circle equation and determine when there are:
  6. two intersection points;
  7. one intersection point;
  8. no real intersection points.
  9. Express the classification using \(|c|\).
  10. Explain geometrically why the algebraic boundary between the three cases occurs at \(|c|=2\).
  11. State which of the cases corresponds to a tangent line.

Required record

Include complete calculations for the three numerical cases, one combined diagram, the general classification and a geometric explanation.

Verification

Every computed intersection point must be substituted into both the circle equation and the corresponding line equation.
