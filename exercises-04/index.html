Exercise Set 04

The set follows the division intended for the later student workbook:

  • Problems 01–10 — required practice: calculate, draw, convert and verify;
  • Problems 11–20 — developing work: compare parametrizations, justify constructions, interpret eccentricity and audit mathematical models.

Each problem has its own Markdown file. Separate editable solution.md files will be added only in the forkable student workbook.

Problems 01–10 — required practice

Problem 01 — Points of a parametrized circle Generate special points, draw the circle and verify its Cartesian equation.

Problem 02 — Points of a parametrized ellipse Calculate ellipse points, identify semiaxes and check the Cartesian description.

Problem 03 — A parametrized parabola Generate a parabola, eliminate the parameter and mark a restricted trace.

Problem 04 — The parameter interval selects an arc Connect two parameter intervals with the upper and lower semicircles.

Problem 05 — Reading centres and semiaxes Extract centres, scales, vertices, widths and heights from ellipse equations.

Problem 06 — Changing one ellipse parameter at a time Build a parameter-effect table and verify the resulting ellipses.

Problem 07 — Cartesian and radial coordinates Convert points in both directions under the convention \(r\ge0\).

Problem 08 — Reading a radial ellipse Compute radial distances, convert points and identify focal extremes.

Problem 09 — Classifying radial conics Read \(p\) and \(e\), classify four cases and inspect special directions.

Problem 10 — Checking a focus-directrix parabola Compare distances, derive the Cartesian equation and verify selected points.

Problems 11–20 — developing work

Problem 11 — One circle, three parametrizations Compare geometric image, traversal direction and parameter interval.

Problem 12 — A parameter on a curve and parameters of a family Separate the parameter selecting a point from parameters changing the ellipse.

Problem 13 — From parametrization to Cartesian equation Eliminate parameters and distinguish inclusion from full coverage.

Problem 14 — The transition at eccentricity one Compare radial conics below, at and above the boundary value \(e=1\).

Problem 15 — Deriving the radial conic equation Derive the common radial form from a labelled focus-directrix construction.

Problem 16 — Cartesian and focal descriptions of one ellipse Compare centre-based and focus-based representations of the same curve.

Problem 17 — One focus and directrix, three eccentricities Keep the geometric data fixed while changing the eccentricity regime.

Problem 18 — The same ellipse, different motions Compare three parameter rules without confusing them with physical time laws.

Problem 19 — Audit of a trajectory model Classify statements as geometry, parametrization, assumption or unsupported claim.

Problem 20 — A complete conic-model note Combine radial calculations, focus-directrix checks and modelling limits.
