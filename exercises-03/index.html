Exercises · Lecture 03

Lines and Planes

Damian Chorążkiewicz

The lecture develops one line in several representations; the exercises expand the technique, verification and three-dimensional extension without turning the lecture into a catalogue of formulas.

Exercise design

Generating lines

Connection to the lecture

The lecture constructs a line from a point \(P_0\), a nonzero direction \(v\) and the parameter \(t\).

Boundary of the lecture

One line and a few values of \(t\) are shown. Membership tests and alternative parameterizations are transferred.

Work transferred to exercises

Students should generate points, recover an equation from a point and direction, test point membership, determine the parameter of a given point and construct distinct parameterizations of the same line.

Planned problem types

  • direct point generation;
  • point-membership tests;
  • reconstruction from geometric data;
  • comparison of two parameterizations;
  • one drawing or animation task.

Required workbook result

A parameter table, a line drawing, membership calculations and a conclusion explaining why a line has many valid parameterizations.

Verification

At least two generated points must be substituted into every claimed equivalent equation.

Equivalent representations

Connection to the lecture

One concrete line is followed through parametric, slope and normal descriptions.

Boundary of the lecture

The lecture performs only one full conversion. Repeated conversion and exceptional cases belong to exercises.

Work transferred to exercises

Students should:

  1. convert between parametric, slope and normal forms for simple data;
  2. interpret \(m=\tan\alpha\) geometrically;
  3. identify direction and normal vectors;
  4. recognize vertical lines where \(y=mx+b\) is not appropriate;
  5. determine whether two equations describe the same set;
  6. prepare one compact presentation card with three descriptions of one line.

Planned problem types

  • three short conversions;
  • one vertical-line case;
  • one equivalence test;
  • one visual presentation problem.

Required workbook result

Full conversions, a line with its slope triangle and normal vector, and substitution checks.

Verification

Two points generated by the parametric form must satisfy the normal equation.

Intersections

Connection to the lecture

The lecture defines intersection as simultaneous membership and shows one book-style calculation.

Boundary of the lecture

Only one intersecting pair is solved fully. Classification and comparison of methods are transferred.

Work transferred to exercises

Students should solve elementary intersections, check the point in both lines, classify intersecting, parallel and coincident cases, compare parametric and Cartesian methods, and build a decision scheme based on directions and common points.

Planned problem types

  • several small systems with integer solutions;
  • one parallel pair;
  • one coincident pair;
  • one method-comparison problem;
  • one decision-diagram task.

Required workbook result

At least one complete solution containing equations, imposed equality, solution, geometric point and verification in both objects.

Verification

No intersection answer is accepted without substitution into both line descriptions.

Point-line distance

Connection to the lecture

The lecture visualizes the shortest connecting segment and relates it to the normal direction and projection.

Boundary of the lecture

One transparent example is calculated. General practice and inverse questions move to exercises.

Work transferred to exercises

Students should calculate distances, construct perpendicular feet, verify orthogonality, compare the coordinate formula with a projection calculation and solve one inverse locus problem.

Planned problem types

  • direct distance calculations;
  • construction of the nearest point;
  • comparison of two methods;
  • one inverse problem describing points at a fixed distance from a line;
  • one explanatory diagram.

Required workbook result

A calculation, geometric construction, orthogonality test and interpretation of the minimum.

Verification

The foot must belong to the line and the connecting vector must be perpendicular to its direction.

Planes and space

Connection to the lecture

The lecture only extends the generating idea: one point plus two independent directions produces a plane; a normal gives an equivalent condition.

Boundary of the lecture

This is the major transfer point. The cross product, a plane through three points and the full classification of spatial relations are not developed fully during the lecture.

Work transferred to exercises

Students should:

  1. generate and test points of a parametric plane;
  2. detect when two supposed directions are dependent;
  3. construct two directions from three noncollinear points;
  4. compute a normal with the cross product and verify both dot products vanish;
  5. write parametric and normal equations of one plane;
  6. compare line-plane and plane-plane positions in simple cases;
  7. prepare a three-dimensional visualization or a sequence of labelled views.

Planned problem types

  • elementary point-generation tasks;
  • one dependence test;
  • one full plane-through-three-points problem;
  • one line-plane intersection;
  • one visual or presentation task.

Required workbook result

Full construction from three points, parametric and normal equations, cross-product checks, a diagram and a short classification argument.

Verification

All three original points must satisfy the final plane equation, and the normal must be orthogonal to both generating directions.

Set audit

The exercise set must preserve the lecture’s hierarchy: the line is the primary object, while the plane is a controlled extension. Spatial technique may be substantial in the workbook, but it must always point back to the same generating principle rather than become an unrelated formula list.
